Five ways to render a comma-separated email list

The field is a Long Text Area holding addresses joined by commas. The standard textarea shows two and a half lines of wrapped text with the rest clipped, so at twenty entries nobody can tell who is on the case. Every option below parses the same string on commas. None of them change how the value is stored.

Sample data is invented. Domain shapes, role mailboxes and the internal / external mix mirror real cases, but no address here belongs to a real person or customer. This file is committed to a public repository.

What it does today

Fixed-height textarea, mid-word wrapping, third line cut through the letterforms. The count is invisible, so is any structure.

Two facts drive every option: the median list is short enough to show in full, and the long ones cluster heavily by domain. A design tuned only for twenty entries wastes space on the common case.

01

Chips with an overflow count

One chip per address, wrapped to two rows. Anything past the second row collapses behind a count that expands in place. Removal moves from text editing to a single click.

Reads
Every address stays legible and individually addressable. No truncation mid-word.
At 20
Shows roughly 6, hides 14 behind the count. Contained, but the expanded state is tall.
At 3
All three visible on one row. Costs less height than today's textarea.
Watch
Long addresses need ellipsis, so the full value lives in a title attribute only.

No Apex, reuses the isUrlList pattern

02

Grouped by organisation

Split each address on the at-sign and group by domain. One collapsed row per organisation with a count, internal staff pinned to the top. The twenty-entry case becomes five rows.

Reads
Answers the actual question, which parties are on this case, before you read a single address.
At 20
Five rows, about 130px. The strongest option at the top of the range by a wide margin.
At 3
Three groups of one. Grouping adds a layer that earns nothing. Needs a threshold to fall back to option 01.
Watch
Domain is not organisation. Regional domains split one company into several rows unless you fold subdomains.

No Apex, pure string work

03

Initial cluster, roster on demand

Collapse the whole list to a row of overlapping initial badges plus a count. Clicking opens a scrollable roster underneath. The field stops competing for height with Status and Priority.

Reads
Presence and scale at a glance. Colour separates internal, external and role mailboxes.
At 20
One 27px row collapsed, capped scroll area open. Cheapest option vertically by far.
At 3
Three badges. Compact, though it hides addresses that would have fitted plainly.
Watch
Initials from an email are unreliable. Role mailboxes give nonsense monograms, and every read costs an interaction.

No Apex, most CSS of the five

04

Resolved against Contact and User

Apex matches each address to a Contact (name plus Account) or, for internal domains, an active User. Resolved entries become names that link to the record. Unresolved ones stay as the raw address, marked as absent from the CRM.

Reads
Real names and account context. Also surfaces something the textarea cannot: this address is on no Contact record.
Measured
5 of 10 sampled addresses matched a Contact. All 4 sampled internal addresses matched a User.
At 3 and 20
Tallest option at both ends. Two lines per entry, so 20 entries needs its own scroll cap.
Watch
Half the rows will look unresolved, which reads as broken unless the empty state is deliberate. Adds an Apex class, a query per render and a new test.

Apex, cacheable query, new test class

05

Summary line, expand to manage

Default to one line of counts. Expanding reveals the managed list, with a paste mode that keeps the raw comma-separated textarea for bulk edits. Reading and editing stop fighting each other.

Reads
Costs one line until someone asks for more. Protects the bulk paste workflow the field was clearly built around.
At 20
One line collapsed. Expanded, it inherits whichever list style you pick above.
At 3
A summary of three is worse than showing three. Hides information that already fitted.
Watch
Not really a fifth design. It is a wrapper, and it needs one of 01 to 04 inside it to be complete.

No Apex, but two states to build and test

Reading the five together

Recommend

Option 01 as the baseline, with option 02 switching on above a threshold.

Chips fit the median list of three with less height than today. Groups earn their keep only in the tail, where nine addresses from one carrier collapse to a single row. One config flag, one threshold, both behaviours from the same parsed array.

Against 04

The resolution idea is sound but the measured hit rate does not carry a layout.

At roughly half unresolved it reads as a broken list. Better as an enhancement layered onto 01 or 02 later: swap in the name when a match exists, keep the address when it does not, and let the display work regardless.

Reusable

Whichever wins, this becomes an isEmailList flag on the JSON config.

AVB_Followers__c is the same shape and gets the same treatment for free. Unlike isUrlList, the stored format stays plain comma-separated text, so Slack and email integrations that write this field keep working untouched.

Open

Three questions the mockups cannot answer.

Does anything downstream parse this field on a strict separator, so that normalising spacing would break it? Should removing a collaborator be possible without opening a modal? And is the group threshold a count, say six, or a distinct-domain count?
